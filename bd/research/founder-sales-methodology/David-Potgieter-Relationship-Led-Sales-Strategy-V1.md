# David Potgieter — Relationship-Led Sales Strategy

**Evidence base:** Source 001 — Dr Shine Ashokan consultation, 19 July 2026

## 1. Strategy Definition

David's commercial method is not conventional hard selling. It is a relationship-led system in which the client experiences insight, authority and value before a substantial offer is made.

The objective is not to pressure a prospect into the fastest transaction. The objective is to become the trusted person who best understands the client's problem, explains the market honestly, creates an achievable path and demonstrates value early.

### Core formula

`Genuine connection -> deep context -> useful teaching -> authority -> reframe -> options -> value up front -> proof -> trusted next step -> long-term relationship`

## 2. Strategic Position

David should position himself as:

- A practitioner who has personally tested the technology.
- A founder who understands operational and commercial consequences.
- A systems builder who knows that structured information and workflows matter more than a fashionable interface.
- An independent advisor who will compare options and identify waste.
- A long-term relationship partner who is willing to prove value before expanding the engagement.

He should not position himself as:

- A generic AI salesperson.
- A developer-for-hire competing only on hourly rate.
- A vendor representative defending one platform in every situation.
- A consultant who sells a large transformation before understanding the client.
- A free resource with unlimited unpriced scope.

## 3. The Ten-Stage Method

### Stage 1 — Establish specific rapport

Find a real shared point: sector, ambition, enthusiasm, problem, background or philosophy.

**Purpose:** Reduce distance and create a human conversation.

**Do:**
- Respond to the person's energy.
- Use their role and context.
- Show curiosity.

**Do not:**
- Use generic compliments.
- force false common ground.
- Begin with a long company pitch.

### Stage 2 — Invite the story

Ask the client to explain what they are trying to achieve, what prompted the enquiry and how the current situation works.

**Purpose:** Discover motivation and allow the client to feel heard.

**Core prompts:**
- “Tell me a little more about what you are trying to achieve.”
- “What would make this genuinely useful in your day-to-day work?”
- “What are you doing today, and where is the real frustration?”

### Stage 3 — Diagnose the wider system

Explore data, people, workflows, constraints, risks, budget, timeline and buying preferences.

**Purpose:** Avoid solving only the visible request.

**Diagnostic categories:**
- Desired outcome.
- Current workflow.
- Data and systems.
- Decision authority.
- Security and governance.
- Cost tolerance.
- Adoption and learning preference.
- Expansion potential.

### Stage 4 — Teach one decisive principle

Give the client a better mental model of the problem.

**Purpose:** Create authority through usefulness.

**Example principle:**
A personal AI assistant should begin with permissions, structured knowledge, workflows and approval rules. Voice is a later interface, not the foundation.

### Stage 5 — Reframe the purchase

Translate the product request into the true outcome.

**Examples:**
- “Build me Jarvis” becomes “create a governed personal intelligence and executive-assistance system”.
- “Hire a developer” becomes “choose the right delivery and governance model”.
- “Automate email” becomes “reduce response friction while preserving human approval”.

### Stage 6 — Establish authority with relevant evidence

Use only the proof that helps the client make the decision.

**Evidence hierarchy:**
1. Direct insight into the client's problem.
2. First-hand implementation lesson.
3. Relevant sector or operational pattern.
4. Demonstrable system, workflow or result.
5. Credential, audience size or client history where proportionate.

### Stage 7 — Offer realistic options

David's natural strength is option-based advisory.

**Standard option structure:**
- **Guided route:** client learns and configures with expert support.
- **Done-for-you route:** iMPLEMENTAi.ae designs and implements the agreed system.
- **Managed hybrid:** iMPLEMENTAi.ae defines architecture and governance while specialist development is sourced or coordinated where required.
- **Staged pilot:** implement the foundation and one to three priority workflows before expanding.

For each option, explain:
- Outcome.
- Client involvement.
- Cost behaviour.
- Time and complexity.
- Main risk.
- Best-fit situation.

### Stage 8 — Give bounded value up front

Provide something immediately useful that proves judgement and capability.

**Suitable forms:**
- Diagnostic insight.
- Short roadmap.
- Template or workflow.
- Design concept.
- Tool recommendation.
- Small proof of concept.
- Market or competitor observation.

**Boundary rule:**
Every free-value item must have a defined purpose, output and stopping point. It should demonstrate the paid path, not replace it.

### Stage 9 — Future-cast responsibly

Show how the first step can lead to a larger result.

**Purpose:** Help the client see the relationship's potential.

**Safe framing:**
- “Once the personal proof of concept is working, we can assess whether parts are reusable for colleagues.”
- “Hospital-level use would require a separate governance and discovery process.”

**Unsafe framing:**
- Suggesting the personal project is cheap or free only because the client may introduce future business.
- Treating the client mainly as access to a larger account.

### Stage 10 — Close softly but clearly

The next step should be specific, easy to understand and proportionate to the relationship stage.

**Good next steps:**
- Review a roadmap.
- Confirm the top three workflows.
- Complete a data and access map.
- Meet at the client's site.
- Approve a limited discovery or pilot.

**Rule:** Soft selling does not mean vague selling. The conversation must still end with a clear action, owner and date.

## 4. Authority Before Offer

David should avoid leading with prices or packages before he has redefined the problem. His authority is strongest when he helps the client understand:

- What they are actually buying.
- Why the obvious route may fail.
- What foundation is required.
- Which parts should be staged.
- Where costs and risks accumulate.
- How success will be measured.

Once the client understands this, the offer is no longer a commodity comparison.

## 5. Storytelling Sales Framework

### Story structure

1. **Context:** What was happening?
2. **Friction:** What did not work or what was misunderstood?
3. **Experiment:** What did David try?
4. **Lesson:** What pattern emerged?
5. **Client relevance:** Why does this matter here?
6. **Recommendation:** What should the client do next?

### Story types

- **Origin story:** establishes enduring interest and credibility.
- **Failure story:** demonstrates honest practitioner learning.
- **Client pattern:** shows sector or operational understanding.
- **Future-state story:** makes the outcome tangible.
- **Relationship story:** explains the long-term value model.

### Discipline

- Use one story at a time.
- Keep it shorter than the client's own explanation.
- Do not use stories with unnecessary legal, ethical or reputation risk.
- End every story with the client-specific lesson.

## 6. Value-Up-Front Strategy

### Purpose

Value up front should do one or more of the following:

- Reduce uncertainty.
- Demonstrate capability.
- Help the client make a better decision.
- Create a visible quick win.
- Build confidence in the working relationship.
- Reveal the need for a structured next phase.

### Selection test

Offer the item only when:

- It is highly relevant to the client's immediate context.
- It can be completed within a controlled boundary.
- It demonstrates a genuine iMPLEMENTAi.ae capability.
- It does not create a regulatory, security or support burden.
- It does not undermine the commercial value of the main work.

### Required written boundary

For every value-up-front item, record:

- What will be provided.
- What is excluded.
- Who must provide inputs.
- Whether revisions are included.
- What happens after delivery.

## 7. Relationship Economics

David's model assumes that strong relationships produce:

- Repeat work.
- Cross-service opportunities.
- Referrals.
- Faster trust in future projects.
- Better access to decision-makers.
- Lower dependence on paid marketing.

### Correct commercial interpretation

Relationship economics justifies investing in trust and proof. It does not justify uncontrolled discounting, unlimited free work or unpriced scope.

### Decision rule

A lower-entry engagement may be appropriate when:

- The client has credible long-term fit.
- The first stage produces a reusable asset or strong proof.
- Scope is tightly controlled.
- The client understands the value being provided.
- Expansion remains optional and is not assumed.

## 8. Qualification Within a Soft-Sell Model

Soft selling still requires disciplined qualification.

### Minimum qualification

- Who is the decision-maker?
- What outcome matters most?
- What is the current cost or friction?
- What data or access is involved?
- What budget range exists?
- What timeline matters?
- What would prevent implementation?
- Does the client want to learn, delegate or combine both?

### Disqualifiers or caution signals

- No realistic budget for the expected outcome.
- Demands for autonomous access to sensitive systems without governance.
- Expectation of unlimited free development.
- Unwillingness to define data ownership or approval authority.
- Desire for guaranteed results unsupported by the available evidence.
- Misalignment between the personal project and hospital or enterprise authority.

## 9. Commercial Guardrails

- Do not promise lowest price or highest value as a guarantee.
- Do not quote an undefined full build merely to satisfy an early price request.
- Do provide a staged commercial route or indicative range once enough scope is known.
- Do not attack a competitor or platform; explain fit, trade-offs and evidence.
- Do not make future referrals part of the current project's implied payment.
- Do not offer a free item without recording its boundary.
- Do not expose confidential clients or internal work as sales proof.
- Do not let enthusiasm override security, healthcare privacy or contractual discipline.
- Record every commitment and promised delivery date.

## 10. Call Quality Scorecard

Score each category from 0 to 5.

| Category | 0 | 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Genuine connection | Generic or absent | Specific, natural and relevant |
| Client discovery | David dominates | Client context, motivation and constraints are clear |
| Reframe quality | Product request repeated | Underlying outcome and system are clarified |
| Teaching value | No useful insight | Client gains a better decision model |
| Authority | Unsupported claims | Relevant evidence and lived experience |
| Options | Single forced route | Clear routes and trade-offs |
| Value up front | Random or unlimited | Bounded, useful and strategic |
| Commercial clarity | Budget and scope ignored | Qualification and next commercial step are clear |
| Restraint | Hype, attacks or overpromising | Strong conviction with evidence and boundaries |
| Next step | Vague ending | Owner, action and date are explicit |

### Interpretation

- **42–50:** Strong David authority conversation.
- **34–41:** Effective but requires targeted refinement.
- **25–33:** Useful content but inconsistent positioning or discipline.
- **Below 25:** Generic, over-sales, under-qualified or reputation-risking.

## 11. Post-Call Conversion Rule

Within the task dashboard, record:

1. Client facts.
2. Client's actual desired outcome.
3. Risks and boundaries.
4. Commitments David made.
5. Value-up-front items offered.
6. Immediate paid opportunity.
7. Future expansion opportunities.
8. Next action, owner and date.
9. Any statements that require verification before being repeated.

The post-call deliverable should convert David's high-energy spoken authority into disciplined written structure.

## 12. Current Working Principle

David's best sales conversations do not feel like sales conversations. They feel like the client has met someone who understands the future, has already made the mistakes, can explain the path clearly and is willing to prove value before asking for a large commitment.

That experience is the sale.
